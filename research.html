Research Overview

Foundation models are becoming part of real-world systems: robots, clinical decision support, industrial inspection, and edge devices. In these settings, the data and requirements keep shifting after deployment, mistakes have real consequences, and compute is scarce. There is still a big gap between AI that looks impressive in a demo and AI you can deploy safely and rely on. Closing that gap is what will let AI deliver on its potential.

Real-world deployment exposes three fundamental limitations in current AI systems: limited adaptiveness (models are fixed once trained, so absorbing new knowledge or feedback requires costly retraining that risks forgetting what they already know), insufficient trustworthiness (models are often overconfident, can be pushed into unsafe outputs, and offer few guarantees of fairness to the people they affect), and resource inefficiency (running and training large models demands more compute and annotated data than many deployments can afford).

My research addresses these limitations through three directions: Adaptive AI, Trustworthy AI, and Resource-Efficient AI, unified by the idea of a Modular AI System (MAS). Rather than retraining one monolithic model for every new requirement, a MAS pairs a shared foundation-model core with lightweight, independently configurable modules: a memory module that absorbs new knowledge, a safety module that steers generation away from harmful outputs, and draft models that reduce the cost of inference. Because these modules leave the core model unchanged, adaptiveness, reliability, and efficiency can be tuned separately for each deployment.

Trustworthy AI
